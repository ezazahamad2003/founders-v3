## 1. Very broad drag‑along; you must sell on terms set by others

### Problematic clauses (excerpts)

**Section 3.2 – Actions to be Taken (Drag‑along mechanics)**

> “If, the Supermajority Holders (the “Electing Holders”) and the Board approve a Sale of the Company in writing, specifying that this Section 3 shall apply to such transaction, then each Shareholder and the Company shall:  
> …  
> (b) **if such transaction is a Share Sale, sell the same proportion of Shares beneficially held by such Shareholder as is being sold by the Electing Holders** to the Person to whom the Electing Holders propose to sell their Shares, and, except as permitted in Section 3.3, **on the same terms and conditions as the Electing Holders**;  
> (c) **execute and deliver all related documentation and take any such other action in support of the Sale of the Company as shall reasonably be requested** by the Company or the Electing Holders…;  
> (e) **refrain from (i) exercising any dissent rights or rights of appraisal**… or (ii) asserting any claim or commencing any suit challenging the Sale of the Company or this Agreement; …”

**Section 3.3 – Exceptions (limited protections)**  
(not quoted in full here, but it only gives narrow limits on reps, indemnities, etc.)

### Why this is problematic for you

- You can be **forced to sell your shares** whenever the Supermajority Holders and the Board approve a Sale of the Company and choose to invoke the drag‑along, even if the price or structure is unattractive to you.
- You must **waive dissent and appraisal rights** and cannot challenge the deal (Section 3.2(e)), removing important statutory remedies if the deal is unfair or conflicted.
- Your obligation to “execute and deliver all related documentation” is open‑ended; you could be pressured into **onerous sale documentation**, disclosure obligations, non‑competes, etc., constrained only by the partial carve‑outs in Section 3.3.
- There is no explicit **minimum price or fairness protection** (e.g., “bona fide third‑party arm’s‑length transaction”, “fair market value”, “board fiduciary out”, independent approval in conflict scenarios).

### Proposed redlines

Add pricing/fairness and process protections and preserve limited dissent/claim rights in cases of misconduct. For example:

1. **Condition drag‑along on bona fide third‑party, arm’s‑length deal and minimum consideration standard**:

> “If, the Supermajority Holders (the “Electing Holders”) and the Board **(acting in good faith and in compliance with their fiduciary duties)** approve a **bona fide, arm’s‑length** Sale of the Company in writing, **for consideration that the Board determines in good faith to be fair to all Shareholders**, specifying that this Section 3 shall apply…”

2. **Limit waiver of dissent/claims to non‑fraud situations**:

Replace Section 3.2(e) with:

> “(e) refrain from (i) exercising any dissent rights or rights of appraisal under applicable law at any time with respect to such Sale of the Company **other than in the case of fraud, bad faith, willful misconduct or a material breach of this Agreement by the Company, the Electing Holders or their Affiliates**, or (ii) asserting any claim or commencing any suit challenging the Sale of the Company or this Agreement, **except to the extent based on fraud, bad faith, willful misconduct or a material breach of this Agreement**;”

3. **Tighten the obligation to sign documents**:

Modify Section 3.2(c):

> “…execute and deliver all related documentation **that is customary for transactions of such type and size and that is consistent with the limitations set out in Section 3.3** and take any such other action in support of the Sale of the Company as shall reasonably be requested…”

4. **Add a “no conflict” safeguard** (new subsection in 3.3):

> “(h) Notwithstanding anything to the contrary, no Shareholder shall be obligated to comply with Section 3.2 in connection with any Proposed Sale in which the primary acquiror is a Founder, an Affiliate of a Founder or of any Electing Holder, or a member of management of the Company, **unless such transaction has been approved by a committee of disinterested directors (or, if there are none, by holders of a majority of the Shares excluding such related parties) following receipt of a fairness opinion from a reputable financial advisor.**”

## 2. Extremely broad, irrevocable voting proxy and power of attorney

### Problematic clause

**Section 5.2 – Irrevocable Proxy and Power of Attorney**

> “Each Shareholder constitutes and appoints as the proxies of such Shareholder and grants a power of attorney and mandate to the Chief Executive Officer of the Company … and a designee of the Electing Holders, and each of them (acting together or alone), with full power of substitution, with respect to the matters set forth herein, including, without limitation, **election and removal of persons as members of the Board**, **votes regarding any Shareholder Action**… and **votes regarding any Sale of the Company**… and authorizes each of them to represent and to:  
> (a) vote, if and only if such Shareholder (i) fails to vote … or (ii) attempts to vote … in a manner which is inconsistent with the terms of this Agreement, all of such Shareholder’s Shares in favour of … the approval of the Shareholder Action or approval of any Sale of the Company…; or  
> (b) **take any action necessary to effect Sections 1, 2, 3 and 4 respectively, including, without limitation, to sign, execute, and deliver any share certificate, stock transfer power, contract … shareholder agreement, consent, waiver and other document or instrument necessary or desirable** to effect any transactions contemplated…”

And:

> “Each proxy and power of attorney and mandatary … is coupled with an interest and shall be irrevocable unless and until this Agreement terminates…”

### Why this is problematic for you

- You give the CEO and the Electing Holders’ designee an **irreversible proxy over your voting rights** and power of attorney to sign documents **on your behalf**, whenever they judge your vote is inconsistent or you fail to act in 48 hours.
- The “any action necessary or desirable” wording is very broad; combined with “full power of substitution,” this could allow actions you didn’t specifically foresee (e.g., signing waivers, consents, agreements limiting your rights).
- It lasts until termination of the Agreement (which may be long‑term if there is no IPO or Sale), significantly **displacing your shareholder autonomy**.

### Proposed redlines

Narrow the scope, tighten triggers, and make the power revocable in certain circumstances:

1. **Limit scope to strictly necessary actions**:

> “…with respect to the matters set forth herein, **but solely to the extent necessary to implement votes and consents expressly required under Sections 1, 2, 3 and 4**, including…”

2. **Delete “or desirable” and “full power of substitution”**:

> “…(b) take any action necessary to effect Sections 1, 2, 3 and 4 respectively, including, without limitation, to sign, execute, and deliver any share certificate, stock transfer power, contract … **only to the extent such documents are customary and consistent with the terms of this Agreement**…”

and

> “with full power of substitution” → **delete**.

3. **Increase response time and limit when they can override your vote**:

> “…(i) fails to vote (whether by proxy, in person or by written resolution) (it being understood that failing to execute a written resolution within **five (5) business days** of being requested shall constitute a failure to vote) or (ii) attempts to vote … in a manner which is inconsistent with **a final and binding decision validly approved in accordance with this Agreement**…”

4. **Allow revocation on certain events**:

> “…and, as such, each is coupled with an interest and shall be irrevocable unless and until this Agreement terminates or expires… **or unless such officer or designee ceases to hold such office or capacity or is found by a court of competent jurisdiction to have acted with fraud, bad faith or willful misconduct with respect to this Agreement or the Company.**”

## 3. Supermajority/majority “Shareholder Action” mechanism overrides your vote

### Problematic clause

**Section 2 – Voting Regarding Shareholder Actions**

> “Subject to Section 3,  
> (a) if:  
> (i) in the case of any action that would require a “special resolution”… **one or more Shareholders of record holding in aggregate… more than two-thirds of the votes attached to the outstanding voting Shares (the “Supermajority Holders”);**  
> (ii) in the case of any other action that would require an “ordinary resolution”… the Majority Holders; or  
> (iii) in the case of waiving the requirement to appoint an auditor… the Majority Holders,  
> in any such case, agree by written consent to approve such action (each action, a “Shareholder Action”); and  
> (b) such Shareholder Action has also been approved by the Board, then **all Shareholders shall: (1) vote all of their respective Shares in favour of such Shareholder Action; (2) waive any dissent, appraisal or similar rights…; and (3) execute and deliver all resolutions, consents and all other similar instruments in favour of such Shareholder Action.**”

### Why this is problematic for you

- Once the Majority or Supermajority Holders sign a written consent and the Board approves, **you must vote in favour**, regardless of your own assessment of the transaction or resolution.
- You must **waive dissent/appraisal rights** for any such Shareholder Action, which could include major corporate changes (amalgamations, continuances, amendments to Articles, etc.).
- This structure effectively turns the Agreement into a **standing, advance voting power** for the controlling holders against which you have no practical protection.

### Proposed redlines

1. **Exclude fundamental conflict transactions and personal prejudice**:

Add at the end of Section 2(b):

> “…then all Shareholders shall… **provided that no Shareholder shall be required to so vote or waive dissent, appraisal or similar rights in respect of any Shareholder Action that (x) constitutes a related‑party transaction primarily benefiting the Supermajority Holders, the Majority Holders, any Founder or their Affiliates, or (y) would result in such Shareholder receiving consideration or rights that are materially less favourable than those received by other holders of the same class or series of Shares.**”

2. **Limit waiver of dissent rights**:

Amend clause 2(b)(2):

> “(2) waive any dissent, appraisal or similar rights to which they may be entitled with respect to such Shareholder Action… **except to the extent based on fraud, bad faith, willful misconduct, or a breach of this Agreement or applicable law by the Company, the Board or the approving Shareholders;**”

3. **Clarify that documentation must be customary and not more onerous than others**:

Add to 2(b)(3):

> “…execute and deliver all resolutions, consents and all other similar instruments in favour of such Shareholder Action, **in form and substance that is customary for such transactions and that applies to all holders of the same class or series of Shares on a substantially equivalent basis.**”

## 4. Amendment and termination can be imposed by Supermajority Holders

### Problematic clause

**Section 7.12 – Consent Required to Amend, Terminate or Waive**

> “This Agreement may be amended or terminated and the observance of any term hereof may be waived … only by a written instrument executed by (a) the Company; and (b) the Supermajority Holders.  
> Notwithstanding the foregoing:  
> (a) this Agreement may not be amended or terminated and the observance of any term of this Agreement may not be waived with respect to a particular Shareholder without the written consent of such Shareholder unless such amendment, termination or waiver applies to all Shareholders holding the same class or series… in the same fashion;  
> (b) **the consent of a particular Shareholder shall not be required for any amendment or waiver if such amendment or waiver either (1) is not directly applicable to the unique rights of such Shareholder set forth in this Agreement or (2) does not adversely affect the rights of such Shareholder in a manner that is different than the effect on the rights of the other Shareholders holding the same class or series…**”

### Why this is problematic for you

- The **Supermajority Holders can change or terminate the Voting Agreement** without your consent, so long as they treat all holders of your class “equally,” even if the change is significantly adverse overall (e.g., strengthening drag‑along or proxy powers).
- The carve‑out in 7.12(b) is vague; what “not directly applicable to the unique rights” or “not adversely affect … in a manner that is different” means is open to interpretation and may still allow **material dilution of your protections**.

### Proposed redlines

1. **Require consent of adversely affected minority for certain changes**:

Replace 7.12(b) with:

> “(b) Notwithstanding the foregoing, **no amendment, termination or waiver that materially and adversely affects the rights or obligations of any Shareholder (including, without limitation, with respect to drag‑along rights and obligations, voting commitments, powers of attorney, or restrictions on transfer) shall be effective as to such Shareholder without the prior written consent of Shareholders holding at least a majority of the Shares held by all Shareholders who are not Supermajority Holders and who are adversely affected thereby.**”

2. **Clarify “same fashion” to include degree of impact**:

In 7.12(a), add:

> “…unless such amendment, termination or waiver applies to all Shareholders holding the same class or series… in the same fashion, **and to a substantially equivalent extent with respect to economic and governance impact.**”

This gives non‑controlling holders a real veto on significant changes that affect them.

## 5. Broad confidentiality obligation with limited carve‑outs

### Problematic clause

**Section 7.1 – Confidentiality**

> “Each Shareholder shall keep confidential and will not disclose, divulge or use for any purpose (other than to monitor its ownership of the Company) any confidential information obtained from the Company… unless such confidential information (a) is known or becomes known to the public in general… (b) is or has been independently developed… or (c) is or has been made known or disclosed… by a third party…; provided, however, that a Shareholder may disclose confidential information (i) to its lawyers, accountants, consultants and other professionals…; (ii) to any prospective purchaser… if such prospective purchaser agrees to be bound by the provisions of this Section 7.1; (iii) to any existing or prospective Affiliate …; or (iv) as may otherwise be required by law, provided that such Shareholder promptly notifies the Company of such disclosure and takes reasonable steps to minimize the extent of any such required disclosure.”

### Why this is problematic for you

- The permitted uses are **narrow**: “other than to monitor its ownership of the Company.” This may not clearly allow you to use information for:
  - Compliance with **regulatory / fund reporting** requirements (e.g., to LPs, regulators).
  - Internal risk management or valuation, beyond a literal “monitor ownership.”
- The prospective purchaser must **agree to be bound by Section 7.1**, which in practice can be unworkable (buyers usually sign a separate NDA, not a full shareholder‑level clause).
- For “required by law” disclosures, you must “promptly notify” and “minimize disclosure,” which may conflict with regulatory or court orders in short timelines.

### Proposed redlines

1. **Expand permitted use and disclosure to regulators and investors**:

Modify the lead sentence:

> “…will not disclose, divulge or use for any purpose (other than to **evaluate, manage and monitor its investment in the Company and to comply with applicable legal, regulatory, tax and reporting requirements**) any confidential information…”

2. **Permit disclosure to regulators and actual/potential investors under NDA**:

In the proviso, add:

> “(v) to any governmental, regulatory, tax or self‑regulatory authority having jurisdiction over such Shareholder, to the extent reasonably required; and  
> (vi) to such Shareholder’s actual or potential direct or indirect investors, financing sources or limited partners, **subject to customary confidentiality obligations no less protective than those set forth herein**; …”

3. **Relax requirement that a buyer be “bound by Section 7.1”**:

Amend 7.1(ii):

> “(ii) to any prospective purchaser of any shares in the capital of the Company from such Shareholder, **subject to such prospective purchaser entering into a customary confidentiality agreement with terms substantially similar to those set forth in this Section 7.1**;”

4. **Qualify notice for disclosures required by law**:

At the end of 7.1(iv):

> “…provided that such Shareholder promptly notifies the Company of such disclosure **to the extent legally permitted and practicable under the circumstances**, and takes reasonable steps to minimize the extent of any such required disclosure.”

## 1. Drag‑Along: Shareholder Liability in a Sale of the Company

### 1.1. Shareholder representations & warranties in a Sale  
**Relevant clause (Section 3.3(a))**  
A Shareholder must comply with the drag‑along unless:

> “any representations and warranties to be made by such Shareholder in connection with the Proposed Sale are limited to representations and warranties related to authority, ownership, residency for tax purposes, and the ability to convey title to such Shares…”

**Risk**

- The clause is generally protective, but it is **open‑ended**: “related to authority, ownership, residency for tax purposes, and the ability to convey title” could still be drafted in a very broad way in the transaction documents.
- There is no explicit cap or survival limitation on the Shareholder’s own reps. That means reps about, for example, tax residency could survive a long time or be subject to uncapped liability depending on how the sale agreement is drafted.

**Suggested improvement**

Tighten the permitted scope and add caps/survival:

> “any representations and warranties to be made by such Shareholder in connection with the Proposed Sale shall be **solely** with respect to (i) such Shareholder’s due authority and capacity to enter into the transaction documents, (ii) such Shareholder’s valid ownership of the Shares being sold, free and clear of encumbrances (other than those created by the transaction documents or under applicable securities laws), and (iii) such Shareholder’s tax residency **to the extent reasonably necessary to determine withholding or reporting obligations**, and in each case shall be **several and not joint, subject to an aggregate liability cap not exceeding the consideration actually received by such Shareholder in such Proposed Sale, and subject to customary survival periods not exceeding [12–24] months from closing (or such shorter period as may be negotiated for the Company’s representations and warranties in the Proposed Sale).**”

---

### 1.2. Exposure to other shareholders’/Company’s breaches & escrow

**Relevant clauses (Section 3.3(b), (d), (e))**

- 3.3(b):

> “the Shareholder shall not be liable for the inaccuracy of any representation or warranty or covenant made by any other Person in connection with the Proposed Sale, other than the Company (except that and only to the extent that funds may be paid out of an escrow established to cover breach of representations, warranties and covenants of the Company as well as breach by any Shareholder of any representations, warranties and covenants provided by all Shareholders with respect to the Company)…”

- 3.3(d):

> “the liability for indemnification, if any, of such Shareholder… is several and not joint… and … is pro rata in proportion to, and does not exceed, the amount of consideration paid to such Shareholder in connection with such Proposed Sale;”

- 3.3(e):

> “liability shall be limited to such Shareholder’s applicable share… of a negotiated aggregate indemnification amount that applies equally to all Shareholders but that in no event exceeds the amount of consideration otherwise payable to such Shareholder… except with respect to claims related to fraud by such Shareholder, the liability for which need not be limited…”

**Risks**

Positive protections:

- Liability is “several and not joint” and **capped at the consideration received**.
- Fraud carve‑out is standard.

Residual / disproportionate risks:

1. **Company‑level breaches funded via escrow**  
   - The clause allows escrow funds to be used for **both** Company‑level and Shareholder‑level breaches. This is typical, but it effectively makes each Shareholder a “backstop” for Company risk up to their escrowed portion, even if they had no control over those matters.
   - There is no explicit cap on the **escrow size** or clarity that the aggregate indemnity basket and caps apply to Company‑level claims, not just shareholder claims.

2. **No explicit baskets/deductibles**  
   - The Agreement is silent on an overall de minimis, basket or threshold before claims drain escrow. That will be negotiated in the sale agreement, but from the Shareholder perspective, confirming a **single aggregate cap and basket** and “first loss” allocation to insurance (if any) would be protective.

3. **Fraud carve‑out is uncapped**  
   - “fraud by such Shareholder” is reasonable, but “fraud” should be tightly defined and limited to **actual fraud by that Shareholder with respect to their own representations**, not imputed or constructive fraud based on information supplied by others.

**Suggested improvements**

Add explicit caps / baskets and narrow fraud:

> “(b) … except that, and only to the extent that, funds may be paid out of a **single, shared escrow or holdback** established to cover (A) breaches of representations, warranties and covenants of the Company, and (B) breaches by any Shareholder of the **limited** representations, warranties and covenants such Shareholders are required to give pursuant to Section 3.3(a); provided that the aggregate amount payable from such escrow or holdback on account of all such claims shall not exceed **[●]% of the aggregate consideration** and shall be subject to customary de minimis and basket thresholds negotiated in good faith.”

> “(d) … and in any event the liability of such Shareholder for all indemnification obligations in connection with the Proposed Sale shall be **limited to the amount of consideration actually received by such Shareholder (net of any taxes paid or payable on such consideration)**.”

> “(e) … except with respect to claims related to **actual, intentional fraud by such Shareholder in respect of the express representations and warranties made by such Shareholder in the definitive transaction documents, which shall be proven by clear and convincing evidence; for greater certainty, no Shareholder shall have any liability for imputed, vicarious, constructive or equitable fraud, or for any fraud of the Company or any other Person.**”

### 1.3. Forced sale on “same terms” – but with carve‑outs

**Relevant clause (Section 3.2(b))**

> “…if such transaction is a Share Sale, [each Shareholder must] sell the same proportion of Shares… as is being sold by the Electing Holders… and, **except as permitted in Section 3.3, on the same terms and conditions** as the Electing Holders;”

**Risks**

- “Same terms and conditions” is generally protective in a drag‑along, but:
  - Section 3.3(f) and (g) allow **different forms of consideration** for some holders where there are employment or securities law constraints.
  - There is no explicit obligation on the Electing Holders or Company to ensure the dragged Shareholders are **not left with worse net economic or tax outcomes** as a result of “same terms”.

You are forced to sell, but the Agreement does not guarantee that the sale terms negotiated by the Electing Holders (economic, tax, conditionality, timing of payments) will be **fair, reasonable or arm’s‑length** from your perspective.

**Suggested improvement**

Add a fairness constraint:

> “(b) … and, except as permitted in Section 3.3, on the same terms and conditions as the Electing Holders; **provided that such terms and conditions shall be negotiated in good faith and shall be no less favourable, in the aggregate, to the Shareholders as a group than those that would reasonably be expected to be obtained in an arm’s‑length transaction with an unrelated third party.**”

---

### 1.4. Different forms of consideration / cash‑out in lieu of securities

**Relevant clauses: 3.2(f) and 3.3(f)(B)**

- 3.2(f):

> “…if the consideration… includes any securities and due receipt thereof by any Shareholder would require under applicable law (x) [registration/qualification] or (y) the provision… of any information other than such information as a prudent issuer would generally furnish in an offering made solely to Accredited Investors, the Company may cause to be paid to any such Shareholder in lieu thereof… cash equal to the **fair value (as determined in good faith by the Board)** of the securities…”

- 3.3(f)(B): similar cash‑in‑lieu mechanism.

**Risks**

- “Fair value (as determined in good faith by the Board)”:
  - No independent valuation or dispute mechanism.
  - If the acquirer’s securities later appreciate substantially, the cash‑out Shareholder bears a potential **opportunity cost**, yet has no say in the valuation.
- This is particularly sensitive where some shareholders receive stock that could be worth more over time, while others are forced into a cash‑out at Board‑determined “fair value”.

**Suggested improvement**

Add objective valuation mechanics and protection:

> “…an amount in cash equal to the fair value of the securities that such Shareholder would otherwise receive, **such fair value to be equal to the price per security paid by the acquirer to other selling securityholders in the same transaction or, if no such price is determinable, based on the per‑security value used to determine the overall purchase price in such transaction. If such value cannot be readily determined, it shall be determined by an independent valuator mutually agreed upon by the Company and holders of a majority of the Shares to be so cashed out, with the cost of such valuation borne by the Company.**”

Also make clear that this mechanism should not be used opportunistically:

> “For greater certainty, the Company shall not elect to pay cash in lieu of securities under this Section 3.2(f) or 3.3(f)(B) **unless and only to the extent reasonably required to comply with applicable law**.”

---

### 1.5. Shareholder Representative immunity

**Relevant clause (Section 3.2(g))**

> “…not to assert any claim or commence any suit against the Shareholder Representative or any other Shareholder with respect to any action or inaction taken or failed to be taken by the Shareholder Representative in connection with its service as the Shareholder Representative, **absent fraud, bad faith, gross negligence or willful misconduct.**”

**Risk**

- This is broadly standard, but it effectively **insulates the Shareholder Representative** from most liability and legal challenge:
  - Ordinary negligence (even serious but not “gross”) is waived.
  - The Representative can make significant decisions that affect how escrow is used, disputes are settled, etc., with limited accountability.

**Suggested improvement**

Slightly lower the liability threshold and add process protections:

> “…not to assert any claim… absent fraud, **intentional misconduct or gross negligence**… **provided that the Shareholder Representative shall at all times act in good faith and in a manner it reasonably believes to be in the best interests of the Shareholders as a group and shall provide periodic updates to the Shareholders on material decisions affecting their economic interests.**”

## 2. Irrevocable Proxy & Power of Attorney (Section 5.2)

**Relevant clause (Section 5.2)**

Shareholders:

> “constitute and appoint… the Chief Executive Officer… and a designee of the Electing Holders… as the proxies… and grant a power of attorney… with respect to the matters set forth herein… [to] vote… if and only if such Shareholder (i) fails to vote… or (ii) attempts to vote… in a manner which is inconsistent with the terms of this Agreement…”

and:

> “take any action necessary to effect Sections 1, 2, 3 and 4 respectively, including… sign, execute, and deliver any… contract… shareholder agreement, consent, waiver and other document or instrument necessary or desirable to effect any transactions contemplated in Sections 1, 2, 3 and 4…”

**Risks**

- This power of attorney is **very broad** and “coupled with an interest” (expressly “irrevocable” until the Agreement terminates).
- It allows the proxyholders to:
  - Sign contracts, sale agreements, escrow agreements, etc., in your name.
  - Potentially agree to **waivers, consents and other obligations** that may create **additional liability or reduce your rights** beyond what you would have agreed to yourself.
- It is triggered if you fail to vote **within 48 hours** of a written resolution request—this is a short window and increases the chance that the proxy is used by default.

**Suggested improvements**

1. Narrow the scope to **only what is strictly necessary**:
   > “…to sign, execute, and deliver any… document or instrument **that is consistent with the terms of this Agreement and the definitive transaction documents approved in accordance with this Agreement and that does not impose on such Shareholder any obligations, liabilities or restrictions beyond those expressly contemplated herein or in such definitive transaction documents.**”

2. Extend the response window and require notice:
   > “(a)… failing to execute a written resolution within **five (5) business days** of being requested shall constitute a failure to vote… **provided that the Company shall include in such request a clear summary of the action to be taken and shall promptly notify the Shareholder when such proxy is exercised on its behalf.**”

3. Clarify that POA cannot be used to change economic terms:
   > “For greater certainty, the foregoing proxy and power of attorney may not be exercised to alter the **amount, form or timing of consideration or the liability caps or survival periods** applicable to such Shareholder, except as expressly authorized by such Shareholder or as required by this Agreement.”

## 3. Limitation of Liability for Designated Directors (Section 1.5)

**Relevant clause**

> “No Shareholder… shall have any liability as a result of designating a person for election as a director… nor shall any Shareholder have any liability as a result of voting for any such designee…”

**Risk**

- This clause protects shareholders (including potentially controlling ones) from “shadow director” liability for the conduct of their nominees.

As noted above, 7.1’s “as may otherwise be required by law” carve‑out will usually allow that, but it is better practice to make this explicit.

**Market‑practice correction (already suggested under 1.2):**

Explicitly allow use of Company confidential information to enforce rights under the Agreement and applicable law.

---

## 5. Irrevocable Proxy and Power of Attorney (Section 5.2) – ancillary risk

While this is not strictly an IP or data clause, it can indirectly affect the Customer’s control over how its shares and votes are used in transactions affecting IP and data:

- The CEO and a designee of the Electing Holders are given a broad power of attorney to “sign, execute, and deliver any … contract (including, sale agreement, escrow agreement and support agreement)” (5.2(b)) if the Shareholder fails to vote/act or acts inconsistently.

**Risk for Customer:**

- The Company and Electing Holders can execute transaction documents on the Customer’s behalf, potentially including provisions affecting warranties, indemnities, or covenants that might indirectly touch on the Customer’s information or IP obligations (e.g., non‑disclosure, assignment, or non‑compete). Section 3.3 mitigates this somewhat but not entirely.

**Market‑practice correction (tie proxy to limitations in 3.3):**

Amend 5.2(b) to cross‑reference the protections in Section 3.3 so that the power of attorney cannot be used to override them:

> “Any action taken by such proxies pursuant to this Section 5.2 shall be consistent with and subject to the limitations on Shareholder obligations set forth in Section 3.3, and nothing in this Section 5.2 shall authorize any Person to bind a Shareholder to any covenant, representation, warranty or obligation in connection with a Sale of the Company in excess of those permitted under Section 3.3.”

## 6. Summary of Key Customer‑Facing Risks and Fixes

1. **No reciprocal confidentiality obligation on the Company (7.1).**  
   - *Risk:* Customer’s own confidential/IP information is unprotected in this document.  
   - *Fix:* Add mutual confidentiality obligations and notice/assistance mechanisms.

2. **No explicit data‑protection/privacy provisions.**  
   - *Risk:* No contractual controls over handling, security, or breach notification for Customer personal data.  
   - *Fix:* Add a basic data‑protection clause referencing applicable laws and commercially reasonable safeguards, with breach‑notification obligations.

3. **Drag‑along may indirectly pressure assignment or use of Customer IP (3.2, 3.3, 5.2).**  
   - *Risk:* In a Sale of the Company, the power of attorney and drag‑along mechanics could be used to sign documents with broader‑than‑intended IP obligations.  
   - *Fix:* Add explicit language that no Shareholder is required to assign/license its own standalone IP or to modify existing commercial IP agreements, except by separate written agreement.

4. **Use of Company confidential information in enforcement is not clearly protected.**  
   - *Risk:* Company could argue the Customer’s use of information in disputes breaches 7.1.  
   - *Fix:* Clarify that confidential information may be used/disclosed as reasonably necessary to enforce rights under this Agreement and applicable law, with reasonable efforts to seek confidential treatment.
